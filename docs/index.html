Practice Flight
with Nimbus

A gentle, step-by-step VR flight for kids who feel nervous about flying. A friendly cloud explains every sound and bump before it happens — and nothing moves on until your child presses a button.

Tips for grown-ups
  • Let your child sit on a chair (not a swivel chair) and stay close by. The whole experience is seated — the cabin never moves, only the view outside the window.
  • Short and often works best: one or two chapters a day in the week before the trip, ending on a calm note. A full flight takes about 15 minutes.
  • Your child is in control. Every step waits for a button press, and the Break button floats them back to the Cloud Lounge to breathe. Never push them to continue.
  • Practise cloud breathing together outside VR too, so it's ready to use on the real plane.
  • If the view tilts or feels off, hold the Meta button on the right controller to re-centre.
  • Point with a controller and pull the trigger — or with hand tracking, point and pinch.
  • Woojer: connect it to the Quest's headphone jack with a 3.5 mm cable (Bluetooth adds a noticeable delay), and plug headphones into the Woojer — the Quest's own speakers switch off when the jack is used. Press Test to check it before the headset goes on.
  • Start on Gentle rumble. It is strongest at takeoff, in the bumps and at touchdown. The Woojer's own intensity buttons work too.
  • Headphones make the sound 3D: the engines stay outside the window and the wheels under the floor as your child turns their head.

This is a familiarisation tool, not therapy. If the fear is strong, stops your child from travelling, or comes with panic attacks, a child psychologist can help — exposure-based therapy for flying is well established and some therapists use VR as part of it. Meta's guidance is that Quest headsets are for ages 10+ with a parent-managed account.
